# D1 — Functional Liquid AI Barrier-Diagnostic Prototype

**Status:** Working local prototype with a synthetic demonstration and one manually curated real-evidence case. The sections below retain planned extensions and research questions.

**Goal:** Build a small, runnable Python demo that uses Liquid AI Decision Models to estimate why a group is not taking a specified action, while making uncertainty and human review explicit.

**Audience:** A developer maintaining the public GitHub example and a facilitator reviewing its output.

## 1. Executive summary

D1 is a decision-support demo, not an autonomous persuader and not a causal-discovery system. The working prototype lets a facilitator select a prepared case: a fictional demonstration or a manually curated case about abstention in the 2009 European Parliament election. The curated case combines a 2009 survey aggregate with paraphrased themes from 2012 focus groups; it does not ingest raw reports. In live mode, the app sends the selected structured case to Liquid AI System One, receives typed decision outputs, applies simple local rules, and shows a hypothesis profile for human review. Mock and live run modes are independent of case selection.

The core diagnostic hypotheses are:

1. **Perception gap:** relevant facts, scale, visibility, or causal relationships are misunderstood or missing.
2. **Values conflict:** stakeholders may understand the same facts but disagree about priorities, principles, trade-offs, or who should bear the cost.
3. **Response-efficacy gap:** stakeholders doubt that the proposed action will produce a meaningful result.
4. **Collective-efficacy gap:** stakeholders doubt that the group can coordinate or influence the result together.
5. **Structural/capability barrier:** time, resources, authority, access, safety, or skills prevent action even if people agree and believe the action could work.
6. **Insufficient evidence:** the case does not support a responsible distinction among these hypotheses.

These causes can coexist. The app must not force them into one exclusive label. Its output is a set of hypotheses and probabilities, not a declaration of the exact cause. Liquid's documentation describes Noul, Choice, and Score as typed decision primitives and shows that multiple questions can be evaluated in a single System One call.[1][2]

## 2. Product boundary

### User story

> As a facilitator, I want to enter an anonymized situation where a group is not taking an intended action, see several plausible barriers and the evidence gaps, and decide what to investigate or try next.

### In scope for the first GitHub demo

- One case at a time.
- Focus-group summaries and aggregate survey results are the primary evidence sources.
- A synthetic fixture and one bounded curated structured case manually prepared from two published European Parliament reports; raw transcripts, recordings, PDFs, archives, and respondent-level survey data are not ingested.
- A local Streamlit interface.
- A real Liquid API call when the user supplies an API key.
- A clearly labeled mock/demo mode for viewing the UI and running tests without a key.
- Independent Noul hypotheses for the candidate barriers.
- Optional Score and Choice questions to demonstrate the other decision primitives.
- Simple, inspectable triage rules.
- Human review before any intervention.
- Synthetic demonstration data plus the bounded curated aggregate case in the repository; source PDFs and archives remain local and ignored.
- No database or user account system in the first version.

### Out of scope

- Raw PDF/archive parsing, transcript or recording ingestion, and respondent-level survey uploads.
- Automatically messaging or targeting people.
- Individual psychological, political, or vulnerability profiles.
- Claiming that the model has found the objectively true cause.
- Generating free-form explanations or persuasive copy from a decision model.
- Production deployment, multi-tenant accounts, analytics infrastructure, or high-stakes decisions.
- Inferring causal impact from one observational case.

## 3. Important methodological limit

A classifier can estimate whether the supplied case resembles a defined category. It cannot prove that the category caused the inaction. For example, low perceived efficacy may accompany inaction without being its cause; a missing permission or fear of retaliation may be the operative blocker.

Use wording such as “evidence points toward,” “possible contributing barrier,” or “insufficient evidence.” Do not use “the cause is” or “the model found exactly where.” Causal claims require a subsequent intervention and outcome measurement, not just a single API response.

## 4. Diagnostic taxonomy and observable evidence

Each label needs a definition, positive signals, counter-signals, and an “unknown” rule. Label evidence, not presumed motives.

| Hypothesis | Operational meaning | Evidence to collect | Common misclassification |
|---|---|---|---|
| `perception_gap` | The group lacks or misjudges a relevant fact, magnitude, distribution, visibility, or causal relationship. | Factual estimates compared with reliable observations; what people know; whether the issue is visible; understanding of how the action connects to the outcome. | Treating disagreement over priorities as ignorance. |
| `values_conflict` | People can share the relevant facts but favor different ends, principles, priorities, or distributions of cost and benefit. | Trade-off questions; who benefits and pays; what compromise is unacceptable; a counterfactual asking what each group would choose if the facts were agreed. | Assuming more facts will resolve a normative disagreement. |
| `response_efficacy_gap` | People doubt that the proposed action will produce a meaningful outcome. | Expected effect of the action; prior attempts and results; beliefs about the action-to-outcome link. | Calling lack of resources “pessimism.” |
| `collective_efficacy_gap` | People doubt that the group can coordinate, sustain the action, or influence the outcome together. | Beliefs about coordination; participation expectations; perceived group influence; prior collective attempts. | Confusing group coordination with individual skill. |
| `structural_barrier` | The action is blocked by objective conditions such as time, resources, authority, access, safety, or required skills. | Process constraints; permission requirements; available resources; opportunity to act; risk of punishment. | Treating a real constraint as a communication problem. |
| `trust_credibility_gap` (later extension) | The message or evidence is discounted because the source is not trusted or has lost legitimacy. | Source history; perceived interests; prior broken commitments; what evidence stakeholders regard as credible. | Sending more of the same message and worsening distrust. |
| `coordination_norms_gap` (later extension) | Individuals may be willing, but do not act because roles, expectations, or social coordination are unclear. | Who should act first; perceived participation by others; role and timing ambiguity; social-risk signals. | Calling a coordination failure a values conflict. |
| `insufficient_evidence` | The available record is absent, thin, contradictory, or not representative enough to distinguish the hypotheses. | Source count and quality; missing stakeholder views; contradictory evidence; survey coverage. | Treating missing evidence as evidence that a barrier is absent. |

### Required distinctions

- **Perception is not disagreement.** Measure what people believe is happening separately from what outcome they consider desirable.
- **Values conflict is not necessarily a failure.** The first useful result may be an accurate map of the disagreement, not consensus.
- **Response efficacy differs from capability.** “This will not work” is different from “I cannot do it” and from “we cannot coordinate.”
- **Perceived efficacy differs from objective opportunity.** A group may believe an action works but be blocked by authority, access, resources, or safety.
- **Trust is a separate possible mechanism.** People can understand a message but reject its source.

## 5. End-to-end user flow

1. The facilitator selects `Synthetic demonstration` or `Curated real evidence` in the sidebar.
2. Independently, they select `mock` or `live`; mock is the offline default, while live requires the user's API key.
3. The app validates the selected structured case and warns against personal or confidential data.
4. In live mode, the app sends that bounded case to Liquid once with several typed questions; mock mode uses the local fixture result.
5. The app normalizes the answers and applies deterministic display/triage rules. A live failure remains an error and never falls back to mock.
6. The facilitator sees separate probabilities, mixed/unknown status, and the next diagnostic probe, then can record a human review in the current session.
7. The wider plan's follow-up evaluation would measure changes in understanding, value disagreement, efficacy beliefs, constraints, and actual behavior; it is not an automated causal conclusion.

## 6. Minimal technical architecture

For a small public example, prefer a single local Python app over microservices.

    Sidebar case selector (synthetic / curated) + independent run-mode selector (mock / live)
        -> structured-case validation and privacy warning
        -> mock fixture OR Liquid adapter (TypeSafe SDK / System One)
        -> response normalizer
        -> local triage policy
        -> results and human-review screen
        -> Streamlit session state (no persistent case database)

The app must distinguish three states:

- `live`: an actual response came from Liquid.
- `mock`: a fixture is being shown because the user explicitly selected demo mode.
- `error`: the live request failed; do not silently substitute mock output.

Responsibilities:

A database is intentionally deferred. If a later pilot needs persistence, add it as a separate decision with retention, access, deletion, and encryption requirements.

## 7. Case input contract

The model receives a compact structured snapshot. Keep source facts, stakeholder interpretations, values, efficacy beliefs, and constraints in separate fields.

### Primary evidence sources: focus groups and surveys

The working curated case concerns abstention in the 2009 European Parliament election and keeps its two source streams distinct: a 2009 post-electoral survey and 2012 abstention focus groups. A researcher manually prepared the bounded structured summary from the published reports. The app selects this record or a synthetic fixture; it does not parse PDFs, archives, transcripts, recordings, or respondent-level files. Do not build those raw-file ingestion paths into the prototype.

For each focus group, preserve:

- Study/group ID, collection date, participant count, recruitment/sampling method, and the moderator prompt relevant to the finding.
- A short theme statement, dissenting views, and optional anonymized quotation snippets with stable evidence IDs.
- Limitations: who was not represented, moderator effects, group dynamics, and whether a theme is based on one or several participants.
- Interpretation rule: focus-group comments can reveal reasoning and themes; they do not estimate how common a belief is in the wider population.

For each survey item, preserve what the source actually reports:

- Exact item wording, response scale, collection dates, sampling frame/method, known population/base, and available counts, denominator, or item nonresponse.
- When counts are published, keep numerator and denominator with each percentage. When only percentages are reported, record the known base and explicitly leave unavailable counts unknown; do not invent respondent counts.
- Preserve rounded percentage values and multi-select semantics. The curated 2009 item allows up to three answers, so its rounded percentages need not sum to 100% and must not be normalized.
- Preserve neutral, missing, and dissenting responses; do not collapse them into a favorable/unfavorable binary without an explicit, tested rule.
- Avoid subgroup reporting where small cells could identify participants.
- Interpretation rule: survey distributions describe reported responses for their stated base; they do not by themselves establish causality or representativeness.

The model state should receive summarized and anonymized findings with their provenance and limitations, not a blended narrative that erases whether a signal came from a focus group or survey. Where the sources conflict, retain both records and mark the conflict for review.

Curated evidence-preparation path:

1. A researcher reviews the published focus-group report outside the app and manually paraphrases selected themes with their method, dates, and limitations; no raw quotations or participant-level data are included.
2. The researcher records the published survey item and its rounded response percentages, multi-select rule, non-voter base, and sampling limitations. No respondent count is inferred when the source facts do not provide one.
3. The two evidence records remain separate and retain their own dates, methods, sources, and limitations; any agreement is an observation, not a causal conclusion.
4. The app sends only this bounded structured case to Liquid in live mode. Raw reports, archives, transcripts, recordings, and respondent-level survey rows remain outside the app.

### Input validation

- Require a concrete action, actor group, observed behavior, and time window.
- Require at least one evidence item or explicitly mark the case as exploratory.
- Keep evidence IDs stable (`E1`, `E2`, …) so the facilitator can refer to them.
- Preserve counter-evidence rather than deleting it to make the case look clearer.
- When survey response counts are present, validate them against the stated valid denominator and preserve item nonresponse separately. For percentage-only aggregates, validate reported values without requiring a 100% sum; preserve rounding, multi-select rules, and the stated base without inventing counts.
- Keep focus-group themes and survey distributions as distinct evidence records, each with its own source ID.
- Do not infer population prevalence from focus-group counts or causal effects from survey association alone.
- Do not include names, email addresses, employee IDs, private messages, or sensitive attributes in the live request.
- The example fixture must be fictional and labeled as such.

## 8. Liquid question design

Liquid documents three decision primitives: Noul returns a yes/no probability, Choice returns a selected option and option probabilities, and Score returns a position over an ordered rubric. The documented Python flow uses `typesafe-sdk`, `TypeSafeClient`, `client.system_one(...)`, and a `state` plus `questions` payload.[1] The Decision Model Guide recommends decision models for bounded outputs; open-ended writing and multi-step explanation remain outside this narrow decision call.[2]

### MVP questions

Use independent Noul questions for:

- `perception_gap`
- `values_conflict`
- `response_efficacy_gap`
- `collective_efficacy_gap`
- `structural_barrier`
- `insufficient_evidence`

Each instruction should say “using only the supplied evidence,” define the construct, and distinguish it from neighboring constructs. Do not ask the model to supply a natural-language chain of reasoning that the API does not return.

### Optional Score question

`response_efficacy_level` can use an ordered rubric such as:

0. Evidence indicates stakeholders expect the action to work.
1. Some doubt, but they still expect a meaningful effect.
2. They expect only a small or uncertain effect.
3. They expect no meaningful effect or describe the action as futile.

The list order defines the scale; the resulting Score is a weighted position over the levels, not a percentage. Add a separate Score for collective efficacy only if the labeled cases show that distinction is useful.

### Optional Choice question

Use Choice for a bounded next diagnostic probe, not for a single “true cause.” Candidate options:

- `check_factual_understanding`
- `map_values_and_tradeoffs`
- `test_response_efficacy`
- `check_collective_coordination`
- `audit_practical_constraints`
- `collect_more_evidence`
- `human_review_mixed_case`

The local triage policy must override this suggestion when evidence is insufficient, the answer is ambiguous, or multiple barriers appear plausible.

### SDK skeleton

This is an implementation outline; verify the current SDK and model availability before pinning dependencies. Liquid's documentation shows `typesafe-sdk`, `TypeSafeClient`, `LIQUID_API_KEY`, the API base URL, and `d1:free` in its examples.[1][2]

    import json
    import os
    from dotenv import load_dotenv
    from typesafe_sdk import Choice, Noul, Score, TypeSafeClient

    load_dotenv()

    client = TypeSafeClient(
        api_key=os.environ["LIQUID_API_KEY"],
        base_url="https://api.liquid.ai",
    )

    result = client.system_one(
        model=os.getenv("D1_MODEL", "d1:free"),
        state=json.dumps(case, ensure_ascii=False),
        questions={
            "perception_gap": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Using only the supplied evidence, is a material gap in "
                    "factual understanding or visibility contributing to the "
                    "inaction? Do not treat a values disagreement as ignorance."
                )
            ),
            "values_conflict": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Using only the supplied evidence, could stakeholders "
                    "share the relevant facts yet disagree about priorities, "
                    "principles, trade-offs, or distribution of costs?"
                )
            ),
            "response_efficacy_gap": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Is there evidence that stakeholders doubt the proposed "
                    "action will produce a meaningful result?"
                )
            ),
            "collective_efficacy_gap": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Is there evidence that stakeholders doubt the group can "
                    "coordinate or influence the result together?"
                )
            ),
            "structural_barrier": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Do objective constraints such as time, resources, "
                    "authority, access, safety, or skills prevent action?"
                )
            ),
            "insufficient_evidence": Noul(
                instructions=(
                    "Is the supplied evidence too thin or contradictory to "
                    "distinguish among the candidate barriers?"
                )
            ),
            "response_efficacy_level": Score(
                instructions="How strongly does the evidence indicate doubt that the action will work?",
                criteria=[
                    "No evident doubt: stakeholders expect the action to work",
                    "Some doubt: they still expect a meaningful effect",
                    "Strong doubt: they expect only a small or uncertain effect",
                    "Futility: they expect no meaningful effect",
                ],
            ),
            "next_diagnostic_probe": Choice(
                instructions="Which single next diagnostic probe is most useful, including a safe uncertainty option?",
                criteria={
                    "check_factual_understanding": "Verify beliefs about the facts and causal relationships",
                    "map_values_and_tradeoffs": "Clarify priorities and unacceptable trade-offs",
                    "test_response_efficacy": "Run a small test of whether the action can change the outcome",
                    "check_collective_coordination": "Clarify roles, timing, and expectations about others",
                    "audit_practical_constraints": "Check resources, time, authority, access, safety, and skills",
                    "collect_more_evidence": "Gather more evidence before choosing an intervention",
                    "human_review_mixed_case": "Escalate a mixed or ambiguous case to a facilitator",
                },
            ),
        },
    )

The code is illustrative and must be exercised against the currently installed SDK. In particular, test how SDK result objects expose each answer before implementing the normalizer. Do not assume return-field names beyond those shown in the official examples.[1]

## 9. Response contract and local triage policy

Normalize the SDK response to an internal structure like this:

    {
      "run_status": "live",
      "model": "d1:free",
      "taxonomy_version": "0.1",
      "hypotheses": {
        "perception_gap": {"probability": 0.0},
        "values_conflict": {"probability": 0.0},
        "response_efficacy_gap": {"probability": 0.0},
        "collective_efficacy_gap": {"probability": 0.0},
        "structural_barrier": {"probability": 0.0},
        "insufficient_evidence": {"probability": 0.0}
      },
      "response_efficacy_level": {"score": 0.0, "probabilities": {}},
      "next_diagnostic_probe": {"choice": "collect_more_evidence", "confidence": 0.0},
      "human_review": {"status": "pending", "override_reason": null}
    }

Local policy should be deterministic and separately tested:

1. If the API failed, return `unavailable`; never disguise an error as a model result.
2. If required evidence is missing or `insufficient_evidence` is above its configured cutoff, show `insufficient` and request more information.
3. If two or more barrier probabilities exceed their configured high cutoff, show `mixed` and require human review.
4. If exactly one barrier exceeds its high cutoff, show it as a leading hypothesis, not a proven cause.
5. If probabilities fall between the configured low and high cutoffs, show `possible / review`; do not force a label.
6. If none exceed the high cutoff, show `no sufficiently supported hypothesis`, not “no barrier exists.”
7. Keep cutoffs in `triage_policy.py` or configuration, never hidden in UI code.

The thresholds are not validated by Liquid documentation and must be selected on a labeled, representative evaluation set. For a toy demo, configurable provisional cutoffs may make the flow visible, but the UI and README must label them “illustrative, not validated.” Noul values are independent and must not be normalized to sum to one.

## 10. User interface requirements

The working UI currently selects a bundled case in the sidebar (`Synthetic demonstration` or `Curated real evidence`) and independently selects `mock` or `live`. It does not yet provide general-purpose case authoring; the form requirements below remain a design target for a later extension.

Minimum screens or panels:

1. **Case form:** desired action, group, observation period, and success definition.
2. **Focus-group form:** study metadata, moderator prompt, themes, dissent, anonymized quote snippets, and limitations.
3. **Survey form:** item wording, scale, available counts or reported percentages, known base, missingness where reported, sampling method, and limitations.
4. **Run mode:** real API or explicitly labeled mock mode.
5. **Hypothesis profile:** separate probability display for each barrier; show `mixed`, `insufficient`, `possible`, `leading hypothesis`, or `unavailable`.
6. **Next probe:** display the Choice suggestion as a suggestion only.
7. **Human review:** confirm/edit/reject and record a short reason.
8. **Safety note:** “This is a hypothesis based on supplied evidence, not a causal finding or a decision about an individual.”

Use a probability bar plus the numeric value; do not use red/green verdict colors that imply certainty. Always display the status of the run (`live`, `mock`, or `error`).

## 12. Testing and validation

### Automated tests

- Schema tests: required fields, malformed evidence, empty actions, Unicode text, focus-group metadata, survey count/denominator consistency when counts are present, and percentage-only aggregate validation.
- Policy tests: one likely barrier, multiple likely barriers, borderline probabilities, insufficient evidence, no supported hypothesis, and API unavailable.
- Normalizer tests: Noul, Choice, and Score result fixtures; missing or malformed fields.
- Privacy tests: reject or redact configured identifiers; ensure secrets do not enter logs.
- Liquid adapter tests: mock the SDK so the standard test suite makes no network calls.
- Optional live smoke test: run only when an API key is configured; do not make this a requirement for ordinary CI.

### Evaluation set

Start with synthetic cases covering:

- A clear perception gap.
- A values conflict with shared facts.
- Response-efficacy doubt.
- Collective-efficacy doubt.
- Agreement and perceived efficacy, but no authority or time.
- A case where a focus-group theme and survey distribution agree.
- A case where focus-group comments and survey results appear to conflict.
- A mixed case.
- An evidence-poor case.
- A contradictory-evidence case.
- A trust/credibility case for the later extension.

Synthetic cases verify software paths, not real-world accuracy. Before making performance claims, collect a representative, anonymized set and have at least two reviewers label each barrier independently as `present`, `absent`, or `unknown`, with evidence IDs. Resolve disagreements explicitly.

Report precision, recall, per-label confusion, calibration/Brier score where appropriate, mixed/abstention rate, human override rate, and inter-reviewer agreement. Inspect costly confusions: values versus perception; efficacy versus actual capability; missing evidence versus absence of a barrier. Do not report only aggregate accuracy.

### Outcome experiment

Record a baseline before intervention and a follow-up after it:

- Perception: factual understanding or estimates against reliable observations.
- Values: which priorities and trade-offs remain in disagreement; consensus is not required.
- Efficacy: expected effect of the action and perceived ability to act individually/together.
- Structural conditions: time, authority, resources, access, safety, and skills.
- Behavior: whether the specified action occurred and when.

A successful classifier is not automatically a successful intervention. Evaluate the diagnostic and the intervention as separate claims.

## 13. Privacy and ethical use

- Keep the repository's synthetic fixture fictional; keep any real-evidence example bounded, manually curated, and sourced from published material. Do not add raw reports or respondent-level data.
- Anonymize any real case before sending it to an external API.
- Verify applicable API data-handling and retention terms before using confidential or personal data; API setup instructions alone do not settle those questions.
- Keep the API key in the backend process or local environment, not in browser code, source control, screenshots, or logs.
- Do not score named individuals or infer sensitive personal traits.
- Do not frame disagreement as ignorance or use the result to manipulate dissenters.
- Require a human facilitator to approve interventions.
- Keep an audit record of model, model name, taxonomy version, thresholds, run status, and human overrides. In v1, this can stay in memory or be exported by the user; persistent storage is deferred.

- A user can select a bundled case and run the app locally; general-purpose case authoring remains a possible extension.
- Mock mode works without an API key and is visibly labeled.
- Live mode calls Liquid through the Python SDK when the user configures a key.
- One System One call returns separate Noul answers and the optional Score/Choice outputs.
- Multiple plausible barriers can be displayed simultaneously.
- Missing/contradictory evidence can result in `insufficient` or `human_review`.
- API errors never appear as valid predictions.
- Human review can override a result and record a reason.
- A case can preserve focus-group and survey evidence as separate, traceable records.
- Survey counts, when present, are checked against a supplied valid denominator; percentage-only multi-select aggregates retain the known base without invented counts or denominators. Focus-group themes are not displayed as population prevalence.
- Tests cover the local policy and response parsing without network access.
- The public repository contains the synthetic fixture and the bounded curated structured case, but no raw reports, archives, respondent-level data, or API key. Local source PDFs and archives remain ignored.
- The UI and README state that results are hypotheses, not proven causes.

## 16. Remaining decisions and validation questions

1. Which additional substantive topics and target actions, if any, should be added beyond the current abstention example?
2. Should a later version support user-authored cases, or remain limited to selecting bundled cases?
3. Which installed/available Liquid model should be used beyond a documentation smoke test? The docs use `d1:free` in examples, but the actual account's availability and terms must be checked.[1]
4. What evidence is permitted to leave the user's environment, and what retention policy applies?
5. What labeled cases and reviewer expertise are available for Spanish-language evaluation?

Do not resolve these by guessing. The first is a product decision; the remaining items can be settled during setup and validation.
